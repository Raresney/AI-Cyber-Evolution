# CYBER-EVO

**Adaptive Red–Blue Co-Evolution for AI Cybersecurity**

CYBER-EVO is a research project investigating how autonomous AI Red Teams and Blue Teams adapt to each other during repeated cybersecurity operations.

The project focuses on the interaction between:

* **AI Red Team** — attack generation, attack-path selection, evasion, persistence, and objective completion.
* **AI Blue Team** — detection, investigation, threat hunting, containment, and defensive adaptation.
* **Cyber environment** — a controlled enterprise-like environment producing security telemetry.
* **Adversarial adaptation** — Red and Blue Teams continuously modify their strategies based on previous interactions.

The primary objective is to study whether continuous Red–Blue interaction produces more robust cybersecurity systems than static, one-shot attack and defense evaluation.

---

## Research Question

> **How does adaptive interaction between autonomous AI Red and Blue Teams affect attack success, defensive generalization, and detection robustness under incomplete and noisy security telemetry?**

---

## Hypothesis

A Blue Team trained or evaluated against a relatively fixed distribution of attacks may become specialized in detecting known attack patterns.

An adaptive Red Team may exploit this specialization by modifying its attack strategies, techniques, execution paths, or observable behavior.

We hypothesize that:

1. Static defensive strategies will become increasingly vulnerable to adaptive attacks.
2. Adaptive Blue Teams will improve their ability to detect previously unseen attack variants.
3. Continuous co-evolution will improve defensive generalization but may also increase false positives, response complexity, and operational cost.
4. Incomplete, delayed, or noisy telemetry will significantly affect the ability of both teams to adapt effectively.

---

## Research Objectives

CYBER-EVO will investigate:

* Adaptive AI-driven attack generation.
* Adaptive AI-driven cyber defense.
* Red–Blue co-evolution over multiple attack/defense rounds.
* Generalization to previously unseen attack techniques and variants.
* Defensive overfitting.
* Detection robustness under degraded telemetry.
* Attack transferability across defensive configurations.
* Changes in Red and Blue strategies over time.
* The relationship between attack stealth and defensive detection.
* The trade-off between detection coverage and false positives.

## System Model

The system consists of four primary components:

```text
                  ┌─────────────────────┐
                  │   Cyber Environment │
                  │                     │
                  │ Windows / Linux     │
                  │ Network Services    │
                  │ Applications        │
                  └──────────┬──────────┘
                             │
                       Security Telemetry
                             │
              ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
              │                             │
       ┌──────▼──────┐               ┌──────▼──────┐
       │  AI Red Team │               │ AI Blue Team │
       │              │               │              │
       │ Attack       │               │ Detection    │
       │ Planning     │               │ Investigation│
       │ Evasion      │               │ Hunting      │
       │ Persistence  │               │ Response     │
       └──────┬───────┘               └──────┬───────┘
              │                              │
              └────────── Feedback ──────────┘
```

The environment provides observations and telemetry to the Blue Team while the Red Team interacts with the environment through controlled attack primitives.

The outcome of each round influences the strategy used in subsequent rounds.

## Co-Evolution

A central component of CYBER-EVO is repeated interaction.

Instead of evaluating a single attack against a static defense, experiments will consist of multiple rounds:

```text
Round 1
   │
   ├── Red attacks
   ├── Blue detects/responds
   └── Results recorded
          │
          ▼
Round 2
   │
   ├── Red adapts
   ├── Blue adapts
   └── Results recorded
          │
          ▼
Round 3
   │
   ├── Red adapts again
   ├── Blue adapts again
   └── Results recorded
          │
          ▼
        ...
```

This allows us to study whether the system:

* converges toward stable strategies,
* produces oscillating strategies,
* causes Red and Blue specialization,
* improves generalization,
* or creates new defensive blind spots.

---

## Experimental Conditions

Experiments will compare multiple configurations.

### A. Static Red vs Static Blue

Baseline condition.

Neither side adapts significantly between rounds.

### B. Adaptive Red vs Static Blue

Measures the ability of an adaptive attacker to exploit a fixed defense.

### C. Static Red vs Adaptive Blue

Measures defensive adaptation without adversarial Red adaptation.

### D. Adaptive Red vs Adaptive Blue

Main co-evolution experiment.

Both teams continuously adapt based on previous interactions.

### E. Adaptive Red vs Adaptive Blue + Degraded Telemetry

Introduces:

* Missing events.
* Delayed events.
* Noisy events.
* Conflicting telemetry.
* Reduced visibility.

This condition investigates defensive robustness under realistic observability limitations.

---

## Unseen Attack Evaluation

A key evaluation will use attack techniques or variants that were not present during the adaptation phase.

The objective is to determine whether the Blue Team learns general defensive capabilities or simply specializes against previously observed Red Team behavior.

```text
Known Attacks
      │
      ▼
Adaptation Phase
      │
      ▼
Blue Team develops defenses
      │
      ▼
────────────────────────
      │
      ▼
Previously Unseen Attacks
      │
      ▼
Generalization Evaluation
```

The primary metric will be **Unseen Attack Detection Rate**.
